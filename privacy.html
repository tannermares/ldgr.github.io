Privacy Policy

Last updated October 29, 2026

The short version: Ldgr never sees your budgets or purchases. They're stored in your own iCloud account. There are no ads, no analytics, and no tracking. Two things do leave your device: crash and error reports, which go to a service called Sentry, and your device's push token, which goes to a small relay that delivers shared-budget notifications. Neither one includes your budgets, purchases, amounts, or names, and the relay can't read the notifications it delivers.

Ldgr is a manual budgeting app for iPhone. This policy explains what happens to the information you put into it, and what doesn't.

What Ldgr stores, and where

Everything you create in Ldgr (your budgets, spending limits, and the purchases you add) is stored in your own private iCloud (CloudKit) database, tied to your Apple Account. If you aren't signed in to iCloud, your data stays on your device.

Your budgets and purchases are never sent to the developer, and the developer has no way to read, copy, or access them. Shared-budget notifications pass through the relay described below, but they're encrypted before they leave the device, and the relay can't read them.

Sharing a budget

When you choose to share a budget, that budget and its purchases are stored in a shared iCloud area that the people you invite can access. Everyone you share with can see the purchases in that budget, and each purchase shows the person who added it. Sharing is handled entirely through Apple's iCloud sharing. You control who is invited and can stop sharing at any time. Only share with people you trust.

Notifications for shared budgets

When someone adds a purchase to a budget you share, Ldgr can send you a notification. To make those arrive quickly, Ldgr uses a small notification relay server, run by the developer and hosted by Render. It's built so the developer can't see anything meaningful about you or your money.

Ldgr registers with the relay when it's installed, so notifications can reach you as soon as a budget is shared. The relay receives your device's Apple push token (needed to deliver any notification at all), a random identifier for your app installation, a random identifier for each shared budget you're in (none, if you don't share any), and each notification's contents already encrypted. The name, amount, and category in each alert are encrypted on the device that added the purchase, using a key that only exists on the participants' devices and in iCloud. The relay never has that key and can't decrypt the message.

The relay can't see your name, any amount, any budget or category name, or your Apple Account. It does know which app installations belong to the same shared budget (by their random identifiers and push tokens) and when a notification was sent. It doesn't keep message contents. Its logs record when a notification was sent and how many devices it went to, using shortened random identifiers. The relay doesn't record IP addresses, though Render may keep standard request logs as its hosting provider.

Muting a shared budget takes it off your registration, so the relay stops sending you its notifications entirely. If you delete Ldgr, Apple stops accepting its push token, and the relay deletes your installation's record the next time it tries to reach it.

Crash and error diagnostics

To find and fix bugs, Ldgr uses a third-party crash and error reporting service called Sentry. Reports are only sent when the app crashes, freezes, or hits an unexpected error. A report contains the app version, your device model and iOS version, the state of the device at the time (things like available memory, battery level, language, and time zone), a random identifier for your installation, and details of the error itself, including a stack trace showing which part of the app's code failed. Sentry also sees your IP address when a report is sent. It uses it to estimate a rough location (city and country), but it doesn't store the IP address itself.

These reports are only used to make the app more reliable. They are not advertising or analytics, and they are not sold. Before a report is sent, Ldgr strips out anything in the error details that could hold your data, so reports do not include your budgets, purchases, amounts, memos, names, or any of the financial information you enter into Ldgr.

What Ldgr does not do

  • No advertising and no ad networks.
  • No usage analytics or behavioral tracking.
  • No selling or sharing of your data with anyone.
  • No account to create. Ldgr uses your existing Apple Account for iCloud sync.

Apple's role

Because Ldgr relies on iCloud to store and sync your data, Apple processes that data on your behalf as part of iCloud. Apple's handling of iCloud data is governed by Apple's Privacy Policy.

Service providers

Ldgr relies on a deliberately short list of providers, and only for the purposes given:

  • Apple (iCloud / CloudKit): stores and syncs your budgets and purchases in your own Apple Account, and handles budget sharing. Governed by Apple's Privacy Policy.
  • Apple Push Notification service: delivers notifications to your device.
  • The Ldgr notification relay: routes shared-budget alerts. Receives only push tokens, random identifiers, and already-encrypted message contents, as described above.
  • Render: hosts the notification relay, including its stored push tokens and random identifiers.
  • Sentry: receives crash and error diagnostics. Never receives your financial data.

Security

Data in transit is protected by TLS. Your budgets and purchases are protected by the security of your own Apple Account, so a strong Apple Account password and two-factor authentication are the most useful things you can do. Shared-budget notification contents are encrypted on-device with a key the relay never holds. No system is perfect. If a breach ever put your data at risk, affected users will be notified.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, export or delete your personal information, and to object to certain processing. Because Ldgr holds none of your financial data, most of this is already in your hands through your Apple Account. For anything that isn't, like a crash report, email privacy@ldgrapp.com and it will be handled. Nobody is treated differently for exercising these rights.

Your control over your data

You can edit or delete any budget or purchase in the app at any time. Because your data lives in your iCloud account, you can also manage or remove it through your Apple Account and iCloud settings. Deleting the app removes it from your device. Data in iCloud is managed through iCloud.

Children

Ldgr is rated 4+ and is safe for all ages, but it's designed for personal budgeting by adults. It does not knowingly collect any personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted on this page with a new "last updated" date. Material changes will be reflected here before they take effect.
